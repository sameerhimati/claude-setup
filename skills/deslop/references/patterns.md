# The tells

Scan every category. The structural and rhythm ones are higher-signal than the
vocabulary ones and are the ones most often skipped.

Each entry is a *candidate*, not a verdict — Step 3 of `SKILL.md` decides.

---

## 1. Vocabulary

Words that survive almost every deletion because they carry no information.

| Tell | Reach for |
|---|---|
| delve into | look at, dig into, or just start |
| leverage (verb) | use — or the verb that happened: migrated, rebuilt, wired |
| utilize / utilization | use |
| robust | say what it withstands |
| seamless / seamlessly | cut it; if it were seamy you would have said so |
| crucial, pivotal, vital, essential | cut it, or say what breaks without it |
| a testament to | evidence of — or drop the clause |
| realm, landscape, arena, sphere, tapestry | name the actual field |
| embark on | start |
| navigate (figurative) | say what the difficulty was |
| foster, cultivate | built, ran, paid for |
| underscore, highlight (figurative) | shows |
| myriad, plethora, a wealth of | many, or the number |
| boasts (of a product/feature) | has |
| spearhead | led — and name who or what |
| game-changer, revolutionary, cutting-edge | say what it does differently |
| holistic, comprehensive, end-to-end | usually deletable whole |
| unlock, supercharge, elevate, empower | the verb that actually happened |
| resonate, deeply | cut |
| at the intersection of | cut |
| in the world of X / in today's X | cut the whole clause |

## 2. Structural

The highest-signal category. These survive vocabulary edits and are what makes
a paragraph read generated even when every word is fine.

- **The antithesis.** "It's not just X — it's Y." "This isn't about X. It's
  about Y." Once per piece is a rhetorical move; twice is a tic. Usually the
  sentence is stronger as just Y.
- **The tricolon.** Three items where the third was invented to make three:
  "faster, cheaper, and more maintainable." Keep the two that are true.
- **The pivot sentence.** A paragraph that ends by restating its own first
  sentence in different words. Delete the last sentence.
- **Connector chains.** Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally / That said /
  Ultimately opening consecutive paragraphs. Most are deletable with no loss.
- **The throat-clear.** "It's important to note that", "It's worth mentioning
  that", "One thing to consider is". Delete the frame, keep the claim.
- **The bookend.** An opening paragraph announcing what the piece will cover and
  a closing one summarizing what it covered. Cut both; start at the first real
  sentence.
- **False balance.** "While X has advantages, it also has drawbacks" with no
  position taken. Either take the position or cut the paragraph.
- **The rhetorical question opener.** "Ever wondered why X?" "What makes a good
  Y?" Answer it in the first line instead, or cut.

## 3. Hedging and padding

- "can help to", "may potentially", "often tends to" — pick one modal or none.
- "in order to" → "to".
- "the fact that" → almost always deletable.
- "a variety of", "a number of", "various" → the number, or nothing.
- "somewhat", "quite", "rather", "fairly" before an adjective → cut.
- Three or more `-ly` adverbs in one sentence → keep the strongest verb.

## 4. Sycophancy and address

- "Great question!", "You're absolutely right", "Happy to help!"
- "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "Let's take a look"
- "As we've seen", "As mentioned earlier"
- Second-person coaching the reader did not ask for: "You'll want to make sure
  that…" → state the fact.

## 5. Formatting

- **Bold scattered mid-sentence** for emphasis on ordinary words.
- **Emoji as section markers** (🚀 ✨ 🔑) in technical writing.
- **Bulleted lists where prose belongs** — a list of three full sentences that
  argue with each other is a paragraph.
- **Header-per-paragraph** — headers on sections too short to need navigating.
- **Em-dash density.** More than roughly one per paragraph reads generated.
  (Note: an em dash is not itself a tell — the *rate* is. Do not strip them all;
  that is its own kind of tell, and it flattens real writing.)

## 6. Rhythm

Checked by reading aloud, not by grep. No fix here is an addition — every one is
a cut.

- Every paragraph within a sentence or two of the same length.
- No sentence under six words anywhere in the piece.
- No sentence over thirty words anywhere in the piece.
- Every section opening with the same move.
- Perfectly parallel list items where the real items are not parallel.

---

## Not tells

Things that pattern-match but are fine, listed so they do not get "fixed":

- Em dashes, semicolons, and the Oxford comma. Punctuation is not evidence.
- Correct spelling and complete sentences.
- Technical terms of art — "robust" in statistics, "delve" about mining,
  "leverage" in finance, "foster" about children.
- Any of the above inside a quotation, a transcript, a citation, or code.
- A construction the author demonstrably already used before this piece.
